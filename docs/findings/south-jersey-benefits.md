# Benefits squeeze in Burlington, Camden & Gloucester counties

_Data as of 2026-10-24. Commentary is hand-written; the tables below the marker are regenerated by
`analysis/district_panel.py` + `analysis/report_counties.py`. Sources: User-Friendly Budgets (2025-26 and 2026-27
budgets), district ACFRs FY2023–FY2025 (audited, Exhibit C-1), Treasury TPAF on-behalf allocations, the October 2026
SEHBP participating-employer list, and the Taxpayers' Guide to Education Spending (students per teacher)._

## What we found

1. **In 2026-27, benefit increases outran state aid in 75 of the 106 districts.** Across the three counties,
   budgeted benefits rose **+$121.5M (+15.4%)** while state aid rose **+$74.0M**, and $21.4M of that aid went to Camden City
   alone. Leaving Camden City out, aid grew about $53M against $120M of new benefit costs.
2. **Health insurance is the benefit.** In the FY2025 audits, health is a median **78%** of the benefits districts pay
   themselves. The rest is mostly PERS pension for non-teaching staff and Social Security. Audited health costs grew a
   median **+17% from FY2023 to FY2025**, before the 34% SEHBP increase that takes effect in January 2027.
3. **Districts on the state plan (SEHBP) budgeted twice the increase:** a median **+26%**, vs. **+14%** for districts on
   private or other plans. Only 13 of the 106 are in SEHBP: Evesham, Willingboro, Pemberton, Camden City, Winslow,
   Camden County Vocational, Elk, Newfield and others (✓ in the tables). Treasury's own report says healthier districts
   leaving the plan is part of why SEHBP premiums are rising.
4. **The most-squeezed districts are mid-size suburban districts, not the poorest:**
   - Burlington: **Evesham** (+$7.2M benefits, −$0.7M aid), Lenape Regional, Moorestown, Mount Laurel
   - Camden: **Cherry Hill** (+$6.9M benefits, +$0.4M aid), Camden County Vocational, Voorhees
   - Gloucester: **Washington Twp** (+$6.4M benefits, +$1.0M aid), Deptford, Kingsway Regional
5. **Kingsway Regional, whose own "Fair Funding" campaign started this debate locally,** loses $0.3M in aid while its
   benefits rise $1.6M. Its audited health costs grew **+30%** from FY23 to FY25, the fastest in Gloucester.
6. **Districts are covering the gap with reserves and property taxes.** Budgeted use of fund balance across the three
   counties drops from $236M (2025-26) to $204M (2026-27). Cherry Hill alone goes from $13.1M to $3.8M, so that cushion is
   running out. The median general-fund **tax levy rose 5.6%**, and 76 of 106 districts rose more than 2%. That's likely
   through the cap law's adjustments, which include one for health care cost growth (still to be checked district by district).

## Limits / next steps
- **Staffing:** students-per-teacher (TGES) only runs through 2024-25, *before* the 2026-27 squeeze, so it can't yet
  show 2026-27 layoffs. Next step: SPR staff counts and district budget-hearing presentations (RIF notices) for 2026-27.
- **Teacher pensions are not in these numbers.** The state pays TPAF pension and retiree medical "on behalf" of districts
  (e.g. $41.9M for Cherry Hill in FY25). It's in the panel (`tpaf_onbehalf_total_2025`) but kept separate, because districts
  don't budget for it.
- **Audit parsing coverage:** health was extracted for 304 of 327 district-years (93%). The gaps are mostly tiny
  non-operating or sending districts (Chesilhurst, Hi Nella, Bass River, Washington Twp–Burlington) and a few unusual
  formats. Four former Abbott districts (Camden City, Gloucester City, Burlington City, Pemberton) book benefits
  differently in their budgets than in their audits, so compare their totals with care.
- The levy-cap health adjustment and SEHBP's 2027 rates are not yet in any district's budget; the 2027-28 budgets (spring 2027) will show them.
