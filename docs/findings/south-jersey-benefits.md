# Benefits squeeze in Burlington, Camden & Gloucester counties

_Data as of 2026-10-24. Commentary is hand-written; the tables below the marker are regenerated by
`analysis/district_panel.py` + `analysis/report_counties.py`. District-by-district evidence is in
[`research/districts/`](../../research/districts/README.md)._

**Method (revised 2026-10-24 after district checks):** benefit growth is measured from the 2025-26 **revised** budget
(as reported in the 2026-27 User-Friendly Budget), and state aid is **NJDOE K-12 formula aid** (FY26 → FY27). The first
draft used the original 2025-26 budget and total state revenue; district documents showed both were off (e.g. Evesham:
+39% → +19.9%, matching the district's own deck).

**Sources for every number on this page** (all public):
[2026-27 User-Friendly Budget data files](https://www.nj.gov/education/budget/ufb/2627/index.shtml) (`approp27.csv` line 71260 benefits, `rev27.csv` levy/fund balance; per-district PDFs at `https://www.nj.gov/education/budget/ufb/2627/reports/<county>/UFB27_<district>.pdf`) ·
[NJDOE FY27 K-12 aid by district](https://www.nj.gov/education/stateaid/2627/FY27_GBM_District_Details.xlsx) ·

[SEHBP participating employers, Oct 2026](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/pensions/documents/pdf/sehbpemp.pdf) ·

or parsed figures with known exceptions; **Low** = indicative only. See [validation](../validation.md).

## What we found

_Figures in findings 1–3 and 5 come from the South Jersey section of [`numbers.md`](numbers.md) and cover the 101
comparable districts unless noted; finding 4 comes from the [district research index](../../research/districts/README.md)
and the linked profiles._

1. **In 2026-27, benefit increases outran formula aid in 74 of the 101 comparable districts.** *Confidence: High.* Benefits rose **+$112.6M
   (+14.5%)** across the three counties' comparable districts, while formula aid rose **+$75.9M**, $22.1M of it to Camden City alone.
   Without Camden City, aid rose $53.8M against $117.5M of new benefit costs (Camden City's own benefits line fell, an accounting move).
2. **Health insurance is the benefit.** *Confidence: Medium-High.* In the FY2025 audits, health is a median **78%** of the benefits districts pay
   themselves (n=94). Audited health costs grew a median **+16.6% from FY2023 to FY2025** (n=89). Districts reported 2026-27 renewals of
   roughly 15–20% from the private joint fund (SHIF) and 25–33% from SEHBP (see the profiles). The ~34% SEHBP increase for January 2027
   ([Sun Papers](https://thesunpapers.com/2026/09/28/nj-school-health-plan-commission-approves-34-premium-hike-for-2027/)) comes on top of that.
3. **SEHBP districts budgeted larger increases** (*Confidence: Medium-Low*: membership is name-matched and only 12 comparable
   districts here are on the list; see [validation V6](../validation.md)): a median **+23.0%** vs. **+15.1%** for districts on other plans. Most
   non-SEHBP districts here buy coverage through SHIF (School Health Insurance Fund) or a broker trust.
4. **District-by-district research (106 profiles):** each profile carries its own confidence rating and fact check, and
   the High/Medium profiles were checked against their sources in an independent adversarial review. **0 Confirmed**,
   **20 Consistent**, **33 No cuts reported**, **53 Insufficient evidence**.
   - **No South Jersey district in our research singles out health costs as the cause of its cuts.** Cherry Hill,
     Evesham and Haddonfield were first rated Confirmed and downgraded in review: each names health alongside other causes.
   - **Consistent** (cuts made, health one stated driver). Examples: Cherry Hill (~70 positions, 14 teacher layoffs; the
     district names health with state aid and transportation), Evesham (7 elementary teaching positions left unfilled and
     an assistant principal; also enrollment and lost incentive aid), Haddonfield (proposed 3.5 teachers and 5 aides;
     adopted cuts unconfirmed), Franklin Twp (13 teachers + 6 support, larger classes), Tabernacle, Chesterfield,
     Collingswood, Haddon Heights, Haddon Twp, Moorestown, Pemberton.
   - **Tax increases were more commonly documented than layoffs.** Many districts used the levy cap's **health-care cost
     adjustment**, often with banked cap, to pass the increase to property taxpayers; the documented amounts are in each
     profile and summed in the [index](../../research/districts/README.md). The median general-fund levy in comparable
     districts rose 5.7%, and 75 of 101 went above 2%.
5. **Planned use of surplus fell.** *Confidence: High* for the totals (UFB line 580). Budgeted fund balance, the surplus districts plan to spend in the year,
   falls from $234M (2025-26) to $203M (2026-27). That is a smaller planned drawdown, not a measure of what is left; separately, at least
   seven Camden County districts' documents say they will use up their excess surplus by June 2027 (see their profiles), leaving less cushion for 2027-28, the first full
   year of the ~34% SEHBP increase.
6. **Kingsway Regional, home of the "Fair Funding" campaign:** a SHIF-projected health increase of +14.14% (~$1.4M; +15.8% is SHIF's average
   renewal), a $1,301,423 health waiver, levy +6.7%, and a reported −9.0 FTE (the final deck's rows sum to −9.84; framed by
   the district as enrollment-driven). See its [profile](../../research/districts/gloucester/2440-kingsway-regional.md). Its Fair Funding page dates from 2016–2018 (pre-S2) and hasn't been updated.

## Limits / next steps
- **Thin evidence for 47 districts.** The session's web-search quota ran out mid-way, and several district sites block bots or
  render documents with JavaScript (Mount Holly, Medford BoardDocs, Glassboro, Maple Shade, Shamong, Westampton,
  Northern Burlington). Those could be retried with the local Playwright scraper.
- **Four districts charge benefits outside the budget's benefits line** (Burlington City, Pemberton, Camden City,
  Gloucester City), so their budget-line growth understates the real cost. They're flagged ⚠️ in the profiles.
- **Non-comparable entities** (Hi Nella, Newfield, Chesilhurst and the special-services/ed-services districts) don't run
  regular schools or get formula aid, so they're excluded from the "comparable" count.
- **Teacher pensions are not in these numbers.** The state pays TPAF pension and retiree medical on behalf of districts
  (e.g. $41.9M for Cherry Hill in FY25). It's in the panel but kept separate.
- **The FY27 aid figures are the Governor's Budget Message numbers**, which is what districts budgeted against; compare with the enacted aid when NJDOE posts it.
- Several districts' plan membership needs checking. Winslow is on the October 2026 SEHBP list but not on the Inquirer's
  September 29 list. Chesterfield's site mentions the NJ Educators Health Plan (an SEHBP plan) but it isn't on the list.
