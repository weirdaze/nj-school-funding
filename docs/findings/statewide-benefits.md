## What we found

1. **Statewide, district benefit budgets rose +$868M (+15.7%) in 2026-27, against +$370M of new formula aid.**
   *Confidence: High* (sums of UFB line 71260 and the NJDOE aid file; 25/25 sampled districts' figures found verbatim in
   their UFB PDFs). In 488 of the 570 comparable districts the benefit increase alone was larger than the aid increase
   (*High* for the count; *Medium* for any single district, see V2–V4 in [validation](../validation.md)). 161 districts had their aid cut.
2. **The squeeze is worst in the suburban north and central counties, where aid barely moved.** *Confidence: High*
   (county sums in the table below):
   - **Bergen:** benefits +$137.5M (+22.4%) vs. aid +$12.9M.
   - **Morris:** +$69.3M vs. +$3.2M.
   - **Somerset:** +$56.7M vs. +$2.2M.
   - **Monmouth:** +$67.3M vs. +$3.1M.
   - **Hunterdon:** +$19.1M vs. +$1.3M.
   In the former Abbott counties (Essex, Passaic, Cumberland, Camden) aid kept pace, mainly through large increases to
   Newark, Paterson, Trenton and Camden City.
3. **Health insurance is the benefit.** Health is a median **78%** of district-paid benefits (FY25 audits, n=483), and
   audited health costs grew a median **+18% from FY2023 to FY2025** (n=506). *Confidence: Medium-High* (audited figures,
   ~91% of district-years parsed; each figure links to its ACFR page).
4. **Districts flagged as SEHBP budgeted larger increases** (median +23.5% vs +15.8%). *Confidence: Medium-Low.*
   Membership comes from name-matching one Treasury list, and some districts' own audits contradict it (e.g. Long Branch,
   Mendham Twp say they're in the State Health Benefits Plan but aren't on the list). Treat this as directional only
   ([validation V6](../validation.md)).
5. **The pressure is going onto property taxes.** The median general-fund levy rose **5.1%**; **454 of 562** districts
   exceeded 2%. *Confidence: High* for the levy figures (UFB), *Low* for attributing this statewide to the health-care
   adjustment: the state files don't break out cap adjustments. The link is confirmed only for districts whose adopted
   resolutions we read ([district research](../../research/districts/README.md)). Budgeted fund-balance use fell from $1.49B to $1.38B (*High*).
6. **Largest gaps between the benefit increase and the aid change**: Franklin Twp (Somerset), Wayne, City of Orange,
   Edison, Livingston, Piscataway, Monroe (Middlesex), Jackson, Old Bridge, Cherry Hill, Hillsborough, Atlantic City.
   *Confidence: Medium* until each is checked against district documents (in progress).

## Caveats
- Some large urban districts charge benefits outside the general-fund benefits line, so their line moves for
  accounting reasons (Newark shows −$34M). Check `benefits_line_incomplete` and the audit figures before quoting individual districts.
- 29 districts revised their 2025-26 benefits budget by more than 10%, and 24 have changes above +40% or below −10%
  ([validation V3–V4](../validation.md)). Don't quote those without district documents.
- Audit parsing covers about 91% of district-years; gaps are mostly scanned, image-only reports and tiny non-operating districts.
- FY27 aid is the Governor's Budget Message figure, which is what districts budgeted against.
