_Data as of 2026-10-24. Commentary is hand-written; the tables below the marker are regenerated by
`analysis/district_panel.py` + `analysis/report_counties.py --counties all`. Method: benefits = UFB line 71260, 2025-26
**revised** → 2026-27 budget; state aid = NJDOE K-12 formula aid FY26 → FY27; health detail from 1,777 district ACFRs

## What we found

_Statewide figures in findings 1–7 come from [`numbers.md`](numbers.md) (generated by `analysis/headline_stats.py`);
finding 8 cites news coverage; finding 9 and the profile examples come from the generated
[district research index](../../research/districts/README.md) and the linked profiles; caveat counts come from
[`validation.md`](../validation.md). "Comparable" = 557
operating districts with a revised 2025-26 baseline and a formula-aid row; 28 non-operating, service-commission and
special-services districts are excluded. An independent adversarial review (another session, 2026-10-24) reproduced the
statewide benefit and aid totals from the raw state files._

1. **Benefits grew while everything else stayed flat.** In comparable districts' 2026-27 budgets, employee benefits rose
   **+15.7% (+$851M)** while **all other general-fund operating spending rose +0.5%**. The median district budgeted
   benefits +17.8% and everything else +0.4%. *Confidence: High* (sums of UFB lines 71260 and 72260; 25/25 sampled
   districts' figures found verbatim in their UFB PDFs). Districts that moved benefits out of the line for accounting
   reasons (Newark, Union City, Camden City, Irvington…) pull the total down; excluding them (n=519), benefits grew +18.1%.
2. **2026-27 is a step up on top of earlier growth.** Adopted-to-adopted benefit budgets grew +8.6%, +5.8% and +8.1% in
   the three prior years, then **+16.7%** into 2026-27. *Confidence: High.* Audited health costs had already risen a
   median **+18% from FY2023 to FY2025** (*Medium-High*; robust to using only fully reconciled audit rows).
3. **New formula aid covered less than half the benefit increase.** Formula aid rose **+$372M** statewide (574 districts
   in the NJDOE file; 167 had cuts). In **476 of 557** comparable districts the benefit increase alone exceeded the aid
   change. *Confidence: High* for the count; *Medium* for any single district. Note this compares one cost line with one
   revenue line; it does not by itself show cuts.
4. **The gap is largest in the suburban north and central counties**, where aid barely moved: Bergen, Morris, Somerset,
   Monmouth, Hunterdon (county table below). In the former Abbott counties aid kept pace, mainly through large increases
   to Newark, Paterson, Trenton and Camden City. *Confidence: High.*
5. **Health insurance is most of it.** Health is a median **78%** of district-paid benefits in FY25 audits (n=447).
   *Confidence: Medium-High.*
6. **Districts on the state plan (SEHBP) budgeted bigger increases**: a median +23.5% (n=143) vs +15.9% (n=414). The gap holds
   **within counties** (median +7.4 points; positive in 15 of 16 counties with enough districts) and **within each
   size tercile**, so it isn't a county or size effect. *Confidence: Medium.* Membership is name-matched to an October 2026
   list that post-dates the budgets, and some districts' audits contradict it ([validation V6](../validation.md)).
7. **Levies rose.** The median general-fund levy rose **+5.0%** and **450 of 557** districts exceeded 2%. *Confidence: High.*
   Budgeted fund balance (UFB line 580, the surplus a district *plans to spend* in the year) fell from $1.47B to $1.36B in
   comparable districts ($1.50B → $1.38B across all districts). That is a smaller planned drawdown; the state files do not
   show how much surplus remains, so this is **not** evidence that reserves are running out. We **cannot** attribute
   the above-2% increases to health costs from state data (the files don't break out cap adjustments). Where we read
   districts' adopted resolutions, many cite the health-care cost adjustment ([district research](../../research/districts/README.md)).
8. **Timing:** these budgets were adopted in spring 2026, before the SEHBP commission's ~34% PY2027 increase (September
   2026; [Sun Papers, Sep. 28, 2026](https://thesunpapers.com/2026/09/28/nj-school-health-plan-commission-approves-34-premium-hike-for-2027/)). The January–June 2027 half of the year was budgeted on an assumption, so actual FY27 benefit spending may run higher.

9. **What districts actually did: 171 district profiles** ([index and evidence table](../../research/districts/README.md)).
   Every Burlington, Camden and Gloucester district plus 65 elsewhere (each county's most- and least-squeezed and median
   district, and the 16 largest statewide gaps). **The sample is not random and over-samples large benefit increases, so
   don't extrapolate these counts statewide.** Four rounds of independent adversarial review checked the High/Medium
   profiles against their cited sources; the counts below are after those corrections.
   - **2 Confirmed** (both High confidence): the district itself names health costs as a cause of its cuts. Piscataway
     (44.5 FTE eliminated, 23 net of additions, mostly by attrition; its budget book attributes them to "the loss of state
     aid, reduction in fund balance, and large increases in health care costs", and it also cites falling enrollment) and
     Livingston (40 positions in the preliminary budget; a 33% premium increase is "the primary driver").
   - **32 Consistent**: cuts made, with benefits as one of several stated factors (state aid, enrollment, salaries,
     tuition, one-time revenue). Examples at High confidence: Bloomfield (24 positions), Franklin Twp, Gloucester (19
     positions incl. 13 teaching), Robbinsville (15 positions), Somers Point (11 positions), Evesham (7 elementary
     teaching positions left unfilled). Six profiles first rated Confirmed (Bloomfield, Evesham, Haddonfield, Somers
     Point, Cherry Hill, Tewksbury) were downgraded in review because the districts give several causes.
   - **50 No cuts reported**, **87 Insufficient evidence** (no usable public documents, documents that don't tie
     anything to health, or proposals that were not adopted).
   - **Health-care cost cap adjustments:** see the index for how many profiled districts documented one and the sum of
     the documented amounts, which mixes adopted, tentative and news-reported figures.
   *Confidence: as rated in each profile.* Only High- and Medium-confidence profiles are listed as examples, and every claim links to its source.

**Bottom line on the claim "benefit costs are forcing cuts, layoffs and larger class sizes":** the cost pressure is
real and large (findings 1–6). In the districts we could document, the usual response was a levy increase using the
health-care cap adjustment, often with fund balance; where cuts happened, districts almost always named health costs
together with other causes. Districts that attribute cuts primarily to health costs are rare in our sample (2 of 171).

## Caveats
- District-level growth rates can be distorted by mid-year budget revisions (29 districts revised 2025-26 benefits by
  >10%) and accounting moves (24 outliers). See [validation V3–V4](../validation.md); don't quote those without district documents.
- Audit (ACFR) figures are used only when the row reconciles to its total (or health is a plausible share of it) **and**
  the audited benefits total is within 15% of the same year's budget-file actual, which catches rows that cover only
  the unallocated part of a district's benefits; 26 implausible rows were rejected outright. Coverage: 447 districts for FY25 health share.
- FY27 aid is the Governor's Budget Message figure; press reports indicate the enacted budget kept the same formula-aid total.
