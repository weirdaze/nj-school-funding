The deck carries a NotebookLM watermark.

- [primary] Cost pressures: health insurance premiums up **16%** and prescription insurance up **30%**, plus "significant rate increases in Energy and P&C Insurance" and salary-guide advancement. Tuition from other districts (send/receive) fell 16%, a loss of **$1,176,803**. Slide 3.
- [primary] State aid: equalization aid of $13.86M (FY26) falls to $13.53M (FY27), described as a "sudden $330K foundational loss." Special education aid stays at $1.95M and security aid at $391K. "State Aid has not kept pace with 16-30% inflationary spikes in healthcare, shifting the structural burden locally." Slide 4.
- [primary] Levy: the general fund levy of $14,587,205 is a "3.15% increase over the revised FY26 budget." It includes a **health care adjustment of $162,630**, "legally utilized to partially offset massive spikes in medical and prescription premiums." "We are maximizing available, legally permitted local revenue to protect core student instruction from further reductions." Slide 5. No banked cap is mentioned.
- [primary] Gap closing: a **$1,246,217 deficit** was closed with high school salary savings of $395,556, "health benefit breakage" of $270,000, substitutes and stipends (MTSS/PLC) of $195,726, middle/Halsted salary savings, paraprofessional and BOE adjustments, and primary/Merriam salary savings of $142,560. The chart labels for the $175,715 and $143,659 bars are transposed, so those two items cannot be assigned reliably. Slide 6.
- [primary] Staffing: "These reductions in force and non-renewals are a reflection of severe, objective financial constraints." Retirements "will not be replaced," "specific instructional and support roles have been eliminated," and support positions were consolidated. Slide 7. Examples: retiring central-office roles absorbed internally; one student assistance counselor now splits time between the high school and Merriam; library services consolidated into "a single District Librarian traveling across all schools"; non-renewed teaching positions covered by "shifting class loads (e.g., merging Engineering and Math instruction)." Slide 8. No position counts are given.
- [primary] Equipment and capital: "All non-essential equipment and capital projects were cut to zero to offset high health, energy, and insurance rates." Slide 11. The district also added $90,000 for paraprofessionals for two out-of-district placements. Slide 10.

## Verification of our numbers
- Benefits: the UFB shows $7,678,239 (2025-26 revised) and $9,116,179 (2026-27), which matches ours (+18.7%). [UFB](https://www.nj.gov/education/budget/ufb/2627/reports/37/UFB27_3590.pdf), p. 4. The district's "$7.8M+" for health/dental is consistent with a $9.1M total benefits line. Its +16% medical and +30% Rx increases bracket our +18.7%.
- Formula aid: the district shows equalization aid down about $330K (UFB: $13,860,378 to $13,534,384, -$325,994). Our -$352,839 formula aid change is consistent; the difference is likely other aid categories. UFB general fund state revenue is $16,579,511 to $16,241,672 (-$337,839), which matches our state_revenue_change_ufb. [UFB](https://www.nj.gov/education/budget/ufb/2627/reports/37/UFB27_3590.pdf), p. 2.
- Levy: $14,141,740 to $14,587,205 is +3.15%, which matches the district and ours (3.2%).
- Fund balance: $250,000 in both years, which matches.
- Minor: the UFB p. 9 "total school levy" is $14,889,810, while slide 13 gives "total local taxes" of $15,111,993 ($14,587,205 + $524,788 debt service). Slide 12 uses $14,889,810, so the UFB figure is probably net of debt service aid.

## Assessment
**Consistent.** Newton closed a $1.25M deficit with reductions in force, non-renewals, unreplaced retirements and consolidated librarian and counselor roles. It explicitly cites 16% medical and 30% prescription premium increases and says state aid "has not kept pace" with them. It also tied cutting equipment and capital to zero directly to health, energy and insurance rates. However, the deficit also reflects a $1.18M loss of send/receive tuition and a $330K equalization aid cut. Health costs are a major stated factor but not the only driver of the staff reductions.

## Confidence
**High**: the key facts come from the district's own budget hearing deck, and our benefits, aid, levy and fund balance figures reconcile with the UFB. Caveats: the deck has no position counts and two transposed chart labels.
